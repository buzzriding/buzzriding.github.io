Zoho Campaigns Got AI Agents and WhatsApp. Here's What's Actually Verified.

Zoho rebuilt its Campaigns platform around AI agent workflows and native WhatsApp sending, starting at $4 a month. Every published account of it, trade coverage included, repeats Zoho's own performance numbers without checking a single one.

BR
The BuzzRiding TeamResearched and refined with AI tools

This is a synthesis of Zoho's own announcement and the trade coverage of it, not a hands-on test. Signing up, connecting an AI agent over MCP, and running a real campaign through it is a fair next step, but it wasn't one this piece could finish inside a single research session. What follows is what Zoho itself says, and what independent outlets have and haven't checked, read directly and cited throughout.

On October 28, 2026, Zoho announced a full rebuild of Zoho Campaigns, its email and multichannel marketing platform. The pitch: faster campaign creation, native WhatsApp sending, and AI agents that can plan and launch a campaign from a plain-language instruction. Paid plans still start at $4 a month.

That is a notable price point for the feature list attached to it. It's also the kind of announcement BuzzRiding has seen before, almost word for word, from a much larger vendor.

📋 How this was put together

Every figure below comes from a source with a URL attached: Zoho's own Campaigns 3.0 migration guide, its AI content documentation, the press release distributed through Marketing Dive, and IT Brief Australia's coverage of the same launch. No first-hand test of the platform is claimed anywhere in this piece.

What actually shipped

Per Zoho's migration guide, the rebuild touches nearly every layer of the product. Sending 100,000 emails is up to four times faster, by Zoho's own internal testing. Importing 100,000 contacts is up to five times faster. Neither figure comes with a published baseline or methodology.

WhatsApp is the real new channel. Campaigns now supports bulk WhatsApp sends using approved templates, trigger-based messages, and WhatsApp steps inside automated workflows, sitting alongside email and SMS with one frequency cap across all three.

The template editor was rebuilt from scratch: theme-level brand colors, a dedicated mobile-design view, dynamic content blocks that vary by audience segment, and cleaner HTML output. Reporting gained unified dashboards per channel, e-commerce revenue tracking, and a Zoho Analytics sync.

The workspace itself changed too, per the migration guide: custom list views, advanced filtering, shareable saved views, and native contact tagging. For a team sharing one account across several people, that's a smaller headline than WhatsApp or AI agents, but it's the kind of detail that shows up in day-to-day use far more often than either.

None of that needed an AI agent to justify. The agent layer is the part getting the press attention, and the part worth slowing down on.

The AI agent layer, in plain language

Zoho Campaigns now supports the Model Context Protocol, or MCP, the same connection standard Anthropic published that lets an AI assistant operate a piece of software directly rather than just chatting about it.

In practice, per Zoho's own documentation, a marketer can give a plain-language instruction and have a connected agent carry out the multi-step task: build a Black Friday campaign for an existing segment, send a test, and schedule it. Or analyze the last ten campaigns, clone the best performer, and adapt it for a new promotion.

A third documented example: find contacts inactive for 90 days, build and tag a re-engagement segment, and prepare a campaign for it. That's a task a marketer could do by hand in an afternoon. The pitch is that the agent does it in minutes, leaving the human to review and send.

That is a real capability, not vapor. It is also, per Zoho's own wording, bounded: results depend on the connected AI tool, the integrations available, and the permissions an admin has configured. Zoho states that marketers remain responsible for campaign decisions. How well any of this works with a specific agent, on a specific account, isn't addressed by Zoho or by the trade coverage of it.

What's free and what costs credits

The headline "$4 a month" is accurate and also incomplete. SMS and WhatsApp carry no additional platform charge, but sending a message through either channel spends purchased credits, at a rate the announcement doesn't spell out in one place.

The AI content tools, including the in-editor Zia Content Analyzer that drafts subject lines and body copy, are built into the template editor rather than gated behind a separate add-on. A free plan remains available for up to 500 contacts and 2,000 emails a month, which is enough for a genuinely small list to test the agent workflows without paying anything.

For a marketer comparing this against a stack of free tools rather than a paid platform, BuzzRiding's roundup of free AI tools for social media marketing is the more direct comparison point than anything paid.

The same gap BuzzRiding already found at HubSpot

This is where the Zoho launch stopped being just another product update. A piece published here in October checked four of HubSpot's own pages making claims about its AI agents and found four different headline numbers, with no shared sample size, customer count, or date range between any of them.

"Two vendors, the same AI-agent pitch, and the same missing methodology both times."

Zoho's numbers follow the identical shape. "Up to four times faster" and "up to five times faster" are both attributed to Zoho's own internal testing, with no baseline given. The agent-task examples are specific and plausible, but no outlet, including Zoho, has published a number for how often an agent completes one of them correctly without a human correcting it.

That isn't an accusation that either company is lying. It's a pattern: AI-agent features are shipping across the entire marketing-tool category faster than anyone is publishing a way to check the claims made about them. BuzzRiding's look at the broader measurement gap found the same hole at the category level, not just at one vendor.

What the existing coverage misses

IT Brief Australia's writeup is the most substantive independent account found for this piece, and it still repeats Zoho's performance figures as reported, attributing them clearly to Zoho but not testing them. It does raise a fair point about handing operational control to an agent, then doesn't assess what happens when a segment or a send time is wrong.

The press-release distribution on Marketing Dive and the regional business-press pickups of the same release add no analysis at all; they're the announcement, repeated. None of the coverage found for this piece puts Zoho's claims next to HubSpot's, or next to any other vendor making the same kind of pitch this year.

Who should actually look at this

A marketer already running email through Zoho's ecosystem, or choosing a first platform on a tight budget, gets a real reason to look: WhatsApp and AI-assisted campaign building at $4 a month plus credits is cheap relative to HubSpot's Professional-tier gate on its own agent features.

Anyone mid-contract with another platform has no reason to switch on these numbers alone. The honest move, for either reader, is the one this site keeps repeating for every vendor making this pitch: pilot one narrow task, track your own before-and-after numbers, and don't budget around a vendor's unsourced average.

💡 Key takeaway

Zoho Campaigns 3.0 is a real, broad rebuild with a genuinely new channel (WhatsApp) and a genuinely new control surface (MCP-based agents). Every performance number attached to the AI layer is Zoho's own, unverified by any outlet this piece could find. Treat it the same way as HubSpot's agent stats: plausible, specific, and unaudited.

The obvious caveat

This piece read public pages and trade coverage; it did not create a Zoho account or run a campaign through an agent. Zoho may hold internal data that supports its speed and adoption claims. None of it is published anywhere this research could find. Treat every number above as Zoho's claim, sourced and linked, not as this site's independent finding.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's new in Zoho Campaigns 3.0?
Per Zoho's migration guide, the rebuild adds native WhatsApp campaigns, AI agent workflows over the Model Context Protocol, a rebuilt template editor, unified cross-channel reporting, and faster email sending and contact imports, among other changes.
How much does Zoho Campaigns cost?
Paid plans start at $4 a month, unchanged from before the rebuild, per Zoho's announcement. A free plan covers up to 500 contacts and 2,000 emails a month. SMS and WhatsApp sending require purchased credits on top of any plan.
What is MCP, in Zoho Campaigns' AI agents?
The Model Context Protocol is a connection standard that lets a compatible AI agent take actions inside an application, such as building or scheduling a campaign, rather than only answering questions about it. Zoho says results depend on the connected agent, available integrations, and configured permissions.
Are Zoho's AI agent performance claims independently verified?
Not based on anything published. Zoho's speed figures come from its own internal testing with no stated baseline. The independent trade coverage found for this piece repeats those figures without testing them.
How does this compare to HubSpot's AI agents?
Both vendors published specific, plausible AI-agent capabilities in 2026 backed by performance numbers with no shared methodology or baseline. BuzzRiding's October review of HubSpot's Agent Hub found the identical pattern at a different price point.
