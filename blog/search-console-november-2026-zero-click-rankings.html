Our Best-Ranking Page Gets 434 Impressions a Month. It Has Zero Clicks.

Our best-ranking page shows up 434 times in 28 days at an average position of 5.4. It has never been clicked.

BR
The BuzzRiding TeamResearched and refined with AI tools

Position 5.4. Four hundred and thirty-four impressions in 28 days. Zero clicks.

That is our single best-performing page by visibility, and by Search Console's own numbers it has never once been clicked in the window we checked. It is not the only one. Five more pages on this site rank well enough to call page one and show the identical pattern: real impressions, a respectable position, and a 0% click-through rate.

We went looking for why, and the honest answer is a mix of something real and something we can't fully prove from this session's data alone.

🔬 How this was built

All figures come from the Search Console Performance report for https://buzzriding.github.io/, Search type: Web (text), the in-app 28-day window (25 Oct–21 Nov 2026). We read the Pages tab at 50 rows and the page-filtered Queries tab, transcribing both by hand into CSV files, alongside a design note written before we drew any conclusions.

The 28 days in four numbers

  • 7 clicks across the whole site.
  • About 1,980 impressions, shown by Search Console as 1.98K.
  • 0.4% click-through rate, the site-wide average.
  • Average position 28.9, roughly page three.

That continues the dip our October edition of this series flagged: September's 14 clicks and 2,650 impressions fell to October's 6 and 1,820, and this trailing 28-day window sits at 7 and 1,980 — still well under the September baseline. But the site-wide average hides the actual story, which lives at the page level.

Finding 1: our single best-ranking page got 434 impressions and zero clicks

5 AI Marketing Certifications Worth It in 2026 averaged position 5.4 across 434 impressions — more than five times the impressions of any other page on the site. Clicks: zero. CTR: 0%.

Broken down by query, two phrasings carry most of that: "which ai marketing certificates actually help with getting a job?" sits at position 2.9 across 118 impressions, and "is an ai marketing certificate worth it" sits at position 6.9 across 86 impressions. Both are top-page-one positions. Both have zero clicks between them.

We nearly treated 434 impressions as a quiet win when we pulled the raw Pages report for this piece. It took the next column — the 0% CTR — to turn it into the actual finding.

Finding 2: it isn't one unlucky page — five more hit the same wall

Filtering the Pages report to position ≤12 and at least 20 impressions (to rule out the single-impression noise our September edition warned about) turns up five more zero-click pages:

  • Do AI Certifications Lead to Promotions? — position 3.6, 21 impressions, 0 clicks.
  • AI Marketing News, Week of August 25 — position 6.2, 34 impressions, 0 clicks.
  • Supermetrics' Claude Connector — position 6.7, 32 impressions, 0 clicks.
  • Constant Contact's Claude Connector — position 8.1, 21 impressions, 0 clicks.
  • AI Marketing Interview Questions — position 11.5, 68 impressions, 0 clicks.

Combined with the certifications page, these six pages account for 610 of the month's 1,980 impressions — 31% of all visibility on the site — for a combined total of zero clicks. That is not one page having a bad month. That is a third of our visibility sitting behind a wall none of it got through.

Finding 3: zero clicks is rare at these positions, not normal

Two independent 2026 studies publish blended click-through rates by position. First Page Sage's report (1,184 domains, March–August 2026) puts blended CTR at 1.7% for position 3 and 0.7% for position 5. Anicca's smaller UK study (six non-brand ecommerce and service sites) puts positions 1–3 around 1.8% and positions 4–6 around 0.8%.

Apply either number to our 434 impressions at position 5.4 and the expected outcome is roughly three clicks, not zero. A quick Poisson estimate puts the odds of landing on exactly zero, if our page behaved like the benchmark, at somewhere around one in twenty — unlucky, but not impossible on chance alone. What makes it worth a second look is that it isn't just this one page: five more pages, independently, show the identical 0% pattern at positions where both studies predict a small but real click share.

"One zero-click page at position 5 could be noise. Six of them, at six different positions, starts to look like a pattern."

Finding 4: AI answers cut CTR further — but we didn't check the SERP this session

Both benchmark studies also report CTR separately for queries where Google shows an AI-generated answer above the organic results. First Page Sage's split is stark: position 5 CTR falls from 2.5% with no AI answer on the page to 0.3% when one is present. Anicca estimates AI Overviews alone cut CTR by roughly 12% on average — a result it flags as not statistically significant on its own — while being cited inside one raises CTR 30–40%.

"Which ai marketing certificates actually help with getting a job?" and "is an ai marketing certificate worth it" are exactly the phrasing AI Overviews tend to answer directly — a yes/no or named-list question that doesn't require visiting a page to resolve. If Google is showing an AI answer on either query, the First Page Sage split would largely explain a 0% CTR at position 5 or 7.

We are flagging this as the likely mechanism, not confirming it. This session kept browser use to Search Console only, so we did not load the live SERP to check for an AI Overview on either query. That check is the first item on our list for the next edition of this series.

Finding 5: this sits inside a bigger, industry-wide shift

Neither benchmark study treats this as unique to small sites. First Page Sage reports that an AI answer now appears on 81.6% of U.S. queries, up from 23.4% a year earlier, and that 74.2% of U.S. searches now end with no click at all, up from 64.8%. Its reported position-1 blended CTR fell from 39.8% in its 2025 report to 7.1% in 2026. Anicca separately found the same query at the same position earned about 11% fewer clicks this autumn than a year before.

Six zero-click pages on a small, year-old blog is a small-sample version of a trend both of these larger, better-resourced studies are independently describing. That doesn't make our result less real. It does mean the fix, if one exists, is not a BuzzRiding-specific fix.

How to run this check on your own site

This needs nothing beyond a verified Search Console property.

  1. Enable the CTR and Position columns in the Performance report — both are off by default.
  2. Sort the Pages tab by impressions and look at CTR for anything with 20 or more impressions and a position in single or low-double digits.
  3. Filter by page URL to see which specific queries are driving a zero-click page's impressions.
  4. Compare against a dated CTR-by-position benchmark rather than a gut feeling about what "should" happen at that rank.
  5. Note the query phrasing. Direct yes/no and "best X" questions are the shape most associated with AI-answer suppression in both studies cited here.

What we are changing because of this

  • Treat an impression-heavy, zero-click page as its own category, not a "give it more time" assumption. Six pages and 610 impressions is too large to file under patience.
  • Check query shape before briefing a direct-question article. "Is X worth it" and "does X help" phrasing is the pattern both cited studies associate with the steepest AI-answer CTR drop.
  • Check the live SERP next session. We're adding a live AI Overview check for these two specific queries to the next Search Console pull, which this session's browser-only-for-GSC scope didn't allow.
  • Keep running this monthly. A one-month zero isn't proof of anything on its own; a repeated zero across three monthly pulls would be.

💡 Key takeaway

Six pages, 610 impressions, zero clicks. Two independent 2026 studies say that shouldn't be the default outcome at these positions — but both also document a search landscape where AI answers increasingly resolve a question before anyone reaches a page at all. Our data can't yet tell us which force is doing the work here.

What this data does and does not tell you

It tells us where this site's visibility concentrates and that a meaningful share of it — roughly a third — converts into zero visits. It tells us that outcome sits outside what two independent, dated benchmark studies would predict for these positions, by a wide enough margin across enough pages that chance alone is a weaker explanation than it would be for any single page in isolation.

It does not tell us whether an AI Overview is actually present on these specific queries — we didn't check this session. It does not prove AI answers are the cause rather than, say, searcher intent that was never going to click through regardless of format. And it cannot separate this site's specific experience from the broader shift both benchmark studies describe industry-wide.

The obvious caveat

One property, one 28-day window, transcribed by hand rather than exported, so a copying slip is possible — the raw CSVs are committed so anyone can check our arithmetic. The two comparison studies are themselves single-vendor reports with disclosed samples of 1,184 domains and six sites respectively; neither is a universal truth, and we've cited them as single data points, not consensus. Most importantly, the AI-answer explanation in Finding 4 is the likeliest mechanism we could find in published research, not a confirmed cause — we have not yet looked at the SERP itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why would a page ranking position 5 get zero clicks?
Two 2026 studies (First Page Sage and Anicca) report average CTR around 0.7-0.8% at position 5, so a small number of clicks would normally be expected from hundreds of impressions. A likely but unconfirmed explanation is an AI-generated answer on the search results page resolving the question before anyone needs to click through.
What CTR should I expect at different Google search positions in 2026?
First Page Sage's 2026 report (1,184 domains) puts blended CTR at roughly 1.7% for position 3 and 0.7% for position 5. Anicca's smaller UK study puts positions 1-3 around 1.8% and positions 4-6 around 0.8%. Both are single-vendor studies, not universal constants.
How much do AI Overviews reduce organic click-through rate?
First Page Sage reports position 5 CTR falling from 2.5% with no AI answer present to 0.3% when one appears. Anicca found AI Overviews alone reduce CTR by roughly 12% on average, while being cited inside one raises CTR by 30-40%.
Is zero-click search increasing industry-wide?
Yes, according to First Page Sage's 2026 report, which found 74.2% of U.S. searches now end without a click, up from 64.8% a year earlier, alongside a rise in AI-answer-covered queries from 23.4% to 81.6% over the same period.
What query phrasing is most likely to trigger an AI answer that suppresses clicks?
Direct yes/no questions and "best X" or "is X worth it" phrasing are the shapes most associated with AI-answer coverage in the studies cited here, because they can often be resolved in a short generated summary without a visit to any single page.
