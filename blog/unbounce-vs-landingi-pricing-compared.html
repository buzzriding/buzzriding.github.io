Unbounce vs. Landingi: How Their Pricing Actually Compares (2026)

"Unbounce vs. Landingi" sends people to this site every week, but nobody has put the two pricing pages side by side. Here's what each one states today, read directly and dated.

BR
The BuzzRiding TeamResearched and refined with AI tools

This is a synthesis, not a hands-on test. BuzzRiding has reviewed Unbounce and Landingi separately, but running both tools side by side on the same campaign would mean two simultaneous subscriptions this outlet does not have. So this article does not claim that. What it does is read both vendors' own pricing pages, on the same day, and report what they actually say.

That turns out to be worth doing. Search Console shows "unbounce vs landingi" earning impressions at position 10.9 over the last 28 days — just off page one — and "landingi vs unbounce" close behind at position 17.9. Both tools already have individual reviews on this site. Neither review puts the two pricing ladders next to each other. This article closes that gap.

📋 How this was put together

Every price and limit below is read directly from each vendor's current pricing page — unbounce.com/pricing and landingi.com/pricing — as published in October 2026. Review-aggregate figures are sourced separately and cited where used. No first-hand testing is claimed anywhere in this piece. SaaS pricing changes without notice, so confirm directly with the vendor before buying.

Unbounce's current ladder: six tiers, and pricing that contradicts itself

Unbounce lists six plans. Starter runs $29/month billed monthly, or $22/month on annual billing — though the Starter section of the page also displays the text "$29 Billed annually" elsewhere, which conflicts with the $22 figure shown for yearly billing. That inconsistency is on Unbounce's own page as of this writing, not an error introduced here.

Starter caps out at 5 landing pages, up to 500 monthly visitors, 1 user and 1 root domain. Build is $99/month monthly ($74 annual): unlimited pages, up to 20,000 visitors, still 1 user and 1 domain, with AI copywriting added. Experiment is $149/month ($112 annual): 3 users, 2 domains, up to 30,000 visitors. Optimize is $249/month ($187 annual): 5 users, 3 domains, up to 50,000 visitors, and adds AI traffic optimization. Concierge and Agency are both custom-quoted, starting at 100,000 and 50,000 monthly visitors respectively, with 15 and 5+ users.

Unbounce's trial is 14 days, full functionality, and the page states no credit card is required to start. The trial account is capped to 1 user, no custom domains, and a reduced traffic allowance.

Landingi's current ladder: four tiers, and a monthly price nobody prints

Landingi lists four plans, all shown at their annual-billing price: Build at $24/month, Optimize at $119/month, Scale from $229/month, and Enterprise from $1,199/month. The page states annual billing works out to two free months versus paying monthly, but does not print what the monthly rate actually is anywhere in the pricing content — a gap Unbounce's page does not have, inconsistent as it otherwise is.

Build includes 10 landing pages, 2,000 monthly visits, 1 custom domain, 2,500 monthly credits and 5 users, plus the Lunar AI page generator and AI assistants. Optimize scales that to 100 pages, 30,000 visits, 3 domains, 10,000 credits and 10 users, adding Solis AI insights. Scale removes the page cap entirely, allows 100,000 to 500,000 monthly visits depending on the exact plan tier, 10 domains, 20,000 credits and unlimited users, and adds what Landingi calls an Orbit MCP server — a way to connect the account to an external LLM. Enterprise goes to unlimited pages, 1 million-plus visits, 100 domains, 60,000 credits, and a feature called Lunar Skill.md that tunes the AI generator to a brand's voice.

Landingi's trial is also 14 days. Unlike Unbounce, the page says a credit card may be requested up front, though it states the account can be cancelled during the trial without being charged. Trial accounts are capped at 200 visits.

Entry tier against entry tier

At the cheapest published price, Landingi Build ($24/month annual) beats Unbounce Starter ($22/month annual) on page count — 10 versus 5 — and on visitor allowance, 2,000 versus 500, for roughly the same monthly cost. Unbounce's cheapest tier is the most restrictive entry point of any tool BuzzRiding has priced in this category so far.

The gap narrows fast once unlimited pages matter more than visitor volume. Unbounce Build, at $74/month annual, already removes the page cap entirely — something Landingi doesn't do until Scale, at $229/month, nearly triple the price. A marketer who needs many page variants but modest traffic gets there far cheaper on Unbounce; one who needs higher visitor caps with a handful of pages gets there cheaper on Landingi.

What third-party reviewers say, for what it's worth

Neither vendor's pricing page is the whole picture, so it's worth checking how each tool is rated independently. On G2, Unbounce holds a 4.3 out of 5 rating across 386 reviews, and Landingi holds 4.5 out of 5 across 387 reviews, both as of this writing. The two tools are rated within a few hundredths of a point of each other on review volume, which suggests comparable adoption — the rating gap (4.3 vs. 4.5) is real but not large enough to treat as a verdict on its own.

"Landingi's cheapest plan outprices Unbounce's on pages and traffic. Unbounce's cheapest plan is also the first to drop the page cap entirely — just two tiers up, not four."

Both vendors now sell an MCP server, buried in the plan tables

One detail is easy to miss in a page-by-page read: both pricing tables mention an MCP server, the protocol that lets an external large language model connect directly to a tool's account. Unbounce includes an MCP server starting on its cheapest Starter tier at $29/month. Landingi calls its version Orbit and reserves it for Scale, its third tier, starting at $229/month.

Neither vendor's page explains in detail what a marketer gains by connecting an LLM directly to their landing-page account rather than using the AI features already built into the editor. That is a reasonable question for a future article, not this one — nothing here claims to have tested either MCP server first-hand. The gap in where each vendor places the feature is still worth knowing before picking a tier: Unbounce bundles it at the bottom of its ladder, Landingi treats it as a mid-tier upsell three price jumps later.

What the comparison sites miss

The pages that currently rank for "unbounce vs landingi" are software-directory listings and small affiliate-style blogs, not an outlet that has reviewed both tools independently. None of the ones checked for this article flagged the pricing-page inconsistency on Unbounce's own Starter tier, and none printed Landingi's actual monthly-billing rate, because Landingi's page doesn't print it either. Both of those are small, specific gaps a generic directory listing has no reason to notice. A publication that has already reviewed both tools does.

Who each tool fits, based on the published limits

A marketer running a single campaign with a handful of page variants and modest traffic fits Landingi Build's limits more comfortably than Unbounce Starter's 500-visitor cap. A marketer who needs many page variants — think a dozen ad-to-landing-page pairings for one campaign — hits Unbounce's unlimited-page tier at $74/month annual, well before Landingi lifts its own page cap at $229/month. An agency managing several client domains gets more domain headroom per dollar from Landingi's Scale tier (10 domains at $229/month) than from Unbounce Optimize (3 domains at $187/month).

None of that is a verdict on build quality, template design or how either editor actually feels to use day to day — those questions are covered in BuzzRiding's separate reviews of each tool, linked below. This article is pricing only.

💡 Key takeaway

Landingi's entry tier buys more pages and traffic for about the same price as Unbounce's entry tier. Unbounce drops its page cap sooner, at the second pricing tier rather than the third. Neither vendor offers a permanent free plan; both offer a 14-day trial, but only Unbounce's states no card is required to start it.

The obvious caveat

SaaS pricing pages change without notice, and Unbounce's own page currently shows two different numbers for its Starter annual price. Treat the figures above as accurate for October 2026 and confirm directly with the vendor before buying. This article makes no first-hand claim about either tool's editor, templates, conversion performance or support quality.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Unbounce or Landingi cheaper?
At the entry tier, Landingi is marginally cheaper on paper ($24/month annual vs. Unbounce's $22–29/month, which the vendor's own page states inconsistently) and includes more pages and traffic at that price. Unbounce becomes the better value once unlimited pages are needed, since it offers that at its second tier while Landingi doesn't until its third.
Does Unbounce or Landingi have a free plan?
Neither has a permanent free plan as of October 2026. Both offer a 14-day free trial. Unbounce's pricing page states no credit card is required to start the trial; Landingi's states a card may be requested, with cancellation available during the trial period.
Which tool has better reviews?
On G2, Landingi holds a 4.5-out-of-5 rating across 387 reviews and Unbounce holds 4.3 out of 5 across 386 reviews, both as of this writing. The gap is real but small, and review ratings don't capture fit for a specific team's workflow.
Which plan removes the unlimited-pages cap first?
Unbounce, at its Build tier ($74–99/month). Landingi doesn't remove its page cap until Scale, starting at $229/month — nearly triple the price.
Has this article tested either tool's builder or templates first-hand?
No. This piece compares only what each vendor's pricing page states. BuzzRiding's separate Unbounce and Landingi reviews, linked above, cover the editors and AI features in more depth.
