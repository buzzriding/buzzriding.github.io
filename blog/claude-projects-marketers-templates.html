5 Claude Projects Every Marketer Should Build (With Ready-to-Copy Prompts)

Every new AI chat starts from zero. You explain your brand, your audience, your voice — again. Claude Projects fix that. Here are the five we built, tested, and would build again first.

Why a project beats a regular chat

A Claude Project is a persistent workspace. You upload files once — a voice guide, a positioning doc, a template — and set instructions once. Every conversation inside that project sees all of it automatically.

Compare that to a regular chat. You paste your brand guidelines in message one, watch them scroll off screen by message ten, and re-paste them next week. Projects remove that tax entirely.

The free tier gives you 5 projects. Pro ($20/month) gives you more, plus better handling of larger knowledge bases. Five is plenty to start — and it happens to match the five below.

⚙️ Before you start

Go to claude.ai/projects → "+ New Project." Name it, add project instructions, then upload files under "Project knowledge." That's the whole mechanic — the templates below are what to put inside.

1. Brand Voice Project

This is the one to build first. Every other project benefits from having your actual voice loaded, so start here.

Files to upload: your brand voice guide, 3–5 examples of your best writing, a short audience description. If you don't have a voice guide yet, we built a free template in our brand voice guide walkthrough — fill it in first, then upload it here.

Instructions to paste in:

  • "Write in short paragraphs, 2–4 lines max. Sentences under 20 words."
  • "Never use: 'game-changer,' 'in today's fast-paced world,' 'unlock,' 'seamless.'"
  • "Lead with the specific insight or number, not a general statement."
  • "When I say 'make it punchier,' that means shorter sentences and stronger verbs — not more adjectives."

Telling Claude what to avoid does more work than telling it what to aim for. Models default to certain patterns; blocking them explicitly forces a different output.

2. Campaign Planner Project

Purpose: turn a one-line campaign idea into a structured brief without starting from a blank page each time.

Files to upload: your last 2–3 campaign briefs (as examples of format), your ICP or persona doc, your approval workflow if one exists.

Instructions to paste in:

  • "When I give you a campaign idea, structure the brief as: objective, audience, key message, channels, timeline, success metric."
  • "Flag any objective that isn't measurable before you build the rest of the brief."
  • "Default channel mix to what's worked in past briefs unless I say otherwise."

This project earns its keep on the second campaign, not the first — that's when the pattern-matching against your past briefs starts producing genuinely useful first drafts.

3. Content Repurposing Project

Purpose: turn one long-form piece into a week of channel-specific content without re-explaining format rules every time.

Files to upload: examples of your best social posts per channel, your newsletter format, any character-limit or hashtag rules you follow.

Instructions to paste in:

  • "From one article, produce: 1 X post (under 280 characters, no link in body), 1 LinkedIn post (150–200 words), 1 newsletter teaser (150 words, one pull quote)."
  • "Keep the core insight identical across formats — vary structure, not substance."
  • "Never invent a statistic or quote that isn't in the source article."

We use a version of this system every week — the full mechanics, including the publishing side, are in our content repurposing workflow.

4. Competitor Watch Project

Purpose: keep a standing summary of what competitors are saying, without a paid monitoring tool.

Files to upload: a running doc of 3–5 competitor URLs and what they currently claim, positioning statements you've collected manually.

Instructions to paste in:

  • "When I paste in a competitor's new page or post, compare it against the positioning doc and flag what's new."
  • "Note gaps: claims they make that we don't, and claims we make that they don't."
  • "Never assume a competitor claim is true — flag it as 'their claim,' not fact."

This won't replace a dedicated tracking tool at scale, but it's free and it's enough for most solo marketers and small teams. Pair it with a manual check once a week.

5. Prompt QA Project

Purpose: a meta-project that improves the instructions you write for the other four.

Files to upload: Anthropic's public prompting guidance, a running list of prompts that worked well for you and ones that didn't.

Instructions to paste in:

  • "When I paste a rough prompt, rewrite it to be more specific: add structure, name the output format, and flag anything ambiguous."
  • "Ask one clarifying question if the goal isn't clear — don't guess."

Run every new project's instructions through this one before you save them. It's the extra ten minutes that turns vague instructions into ones that don't need three rounds of correcting. Our full breakdown of prompt structure — five elements, six templates — is in how to write AI prompts for marketing.

What projects can't do

Files go stale. If you edit your voice guide on your computer, Claude still sees the old upload until you manually delete and re-upload it. Set a monthly reminder to refresh anything that changes.

Projects also can't talk to each other. Your Brand Voice project can't reach into your Campaign Planner project. If both need your audience description, upload it to both — a little duplication beats missing context.

And there's no API access from projects themselves. They live inside claude.ai. For automation that touches other tools, you're looking at Claude's API or a workflow builder, not a project.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a paid Claude plan to use Projects?
No. Free accounts get up to 5 projects with basic file upload, which is enough to build all five templates above. Pro ($20/month) adds more projects and better handling of larger knowledge bases.
How many files can I upload to one project?
There's no hard file count — the real limit is Claude's 200,000-token context window. As a rough guide, a handful of focused markdown or text files works better than one giant document. Keep each project narrow and specific.
Which project should I build first?
Brand Voice. It's the fastest to see a quality difference, and the voice guide you build for it becomes a reusable file you can upload into every other project too.
How often should I update the files in a project?
Monthly is a reasonable default for most marketers — enough to catch positioning shifts and new examples without turning it into a chore. Set a recurring calendar reminder; without one, files quietly go stale.
Can I share a Claude Project with my team?
Only on Team and Enterprise plans, where you can share specific projects and control view or edit access. On free and Pro plans, projects are single-user.
