This Week in AI Marketing: Google Assistant Sunsets, Ads AI Max Goes Mandatory

Two deadlines are colliding at once: your app's voice integrations and your ad account's automation settings. Here's what actually changes and what to check before either one hits.

BR
The BuzzRiding TeamResearched and refined with AI tools

Story 1: Google Assistant disappears from Android on September 4

Google confirmed that Google Assistant starts disappearing from Android devices on September 4, 2026. That's the date the company completes its long-promised transition to Gemini as the default AI assistant across phones, tablets, and connected devices.

This isn't a rebrand. Assistant's older voice-command architecture, including many third-party Actions integrations, is being retired alongside it. Anything your brand built for "Hey Google" flows needs a second look before the switch flips.

📊 The Numbers

Google Assistant retirement date: September 4, 2026. Gemini 3.7 Flash (Google's newest fast model) launched August 13 at half the price of Gemini 3.6 Flash, the pricing move that's making Gemini the default across more surfaces, not just Search.

The timing lines up. Gemini 3.7 Flash landed two weeks before the Assistant cutoff at a steep discount, which suggests Google wants the cheaper, faster model absorbing all that reassigned voice and device traffic without a cost spike on its end.

What the Assistant shutdown means for your marketing stack

If your brand has a voice app, smart-display skill, or any integration that relies on legacy Assistant Actions, test it now. Google has been quietly sunsetting Actions on Google infrastructure for over a year, and September 4 is the hard stop for what's left.

For most marketers, the bigger implication is attention, not code. Gemini becoming the default assistant on every Android device puts it in front of more people, more often, in more contexts than a standalone chatbot app ever could. That's another reason to treat Gemini visibility as a GEO tracking priority, not an afterthought bolted onto your ChatGPT monitoring.

If you haven't compared how your brand's answers actually look across assistants, this is a good week to run that check. We've done head-to-head testing on ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini for marketing tasks, and Gemini's search-surface advantage keeps compounding.

Story 2: Google Ads folds Automatically Created Assets and broad match into AI Max

Google Ads confirmed a September 1 migration that moves Automatically Created Assets (ACA) and campaign-level broad match into AI Max, its automated search campaign layer. After the migration, these settings stop existing as standalone toggles.

In plain terms: features you may have opted into individually, or ignored entirely, get absorbed into one bundled automation layer. If you never explicitly reviewed your ACA or broad-match settings, they're about to get folded into a system with less granular control than before.

⚠️ What To Check Before September 1

Log into Google Ads and review any campaign using Automatically Created Assets or campaign-level broad match. Confirm whether you want that campaign running under AI Max once the migration completes, since opting out gets harder after the cutover.

Should you fight the AI Max migration or lean into it?

Depends on your account. If you've been running tightly controlled exact- and phrase-match campaigns and never touched ACA, this migration barely touches you. If you've been letting Google's automation defaults run unreviewed for months, now is the moment to audit, not after the fact.

The practical move: pull a campaign-level report of anything using ACA or broad match today, decide which campaigns should stay automated and which need tighter guardrails, and make that call before September 1 rather than discovering the new defaults after your spend shifts.

Also worth noting this week

DeepSeek released V4 Flash, a coding-focused model that approaches the performance of Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 while costing roughly 99% less for comparable usage. That kind of pricing gap matters if your team is evaluating AI tools for marketing workflows that lean on coding or automation tasks behind the scenes.

Meta Business Agent launched inside WhatsApp, announced at the company's London Conversations event. Unlike the rule-based chatbots most brands are used to, it holds real multi-turn conversations, checks live inventory, qualifies leads, and can walk a customer through checkout without a human handoff.

The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency rules came into force this month. If you run paid campaigns or AI-generated content into EU markets, this is the provision requiring clear disclosure when content is AI-generated or when users are interacting with an AI system rather than a person. Worth a compliance review if you haven't done one yet.

Microsoft also expanded Agent 365, its internal framework for cataloguing and monitoring AI agents, with Entra Agent ID and Dataverse agent users now in public preview. It's aimed at enterprises managing multiple AI agents at once, a problem more marketing teams will hit as agent-based tools multiply across their stack.

The one thing to do before next week

Pick whichever deadline hits you harder, the Assistant sunset or the AI Max migration, and check it today. Both are dated, both are non-negotiable, and both are easier to handle with a week of runway than a scramble on the day.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does Google Assistant stop working on Android?
Google Assistant begins disappearing from Android devices on September 4, 2026, as part of the company's full transition to Gemini as the default AI assistant. Legacy Assistant Actions integrations are being retired alongside it.
What is Google Ads AI Max, and why is it absorbing ACA and broad match?
AI Max is Google Ads' automated search campaign layer that bundles asset creation, targeting, and matching decisions under one system. Starting September 1, 2026, Automatically Created Assets and campaign-level broad match no longer exist as separate settings — they become part of AI Max by default.
Do I need to do anything before the September 1 AI Max migration?
Only if you've been using Automatically Created Assets or campaign-level broad match without reviewing them recently. Pull a report of affected campaigns, decide which should run under AI Max, and make changes before the migration completes rather than after.
How much cheaper is DeepSeek V4 Flash compared to Claude Opus 4.8?
Reports put DeepSeek V4 Flash at roughly 99% cheaper than Claude Opus 4.8 for comparable coding-task performance. It's a pricing story more than a raw-capability story, and it matters most for teams running high-volume automation rather than flagship creative work.
What does the EU AI Act's Article 50 actually require?
Article 50 sets transparency obligations for AI systems, including requirements to disclose when content is AI-generated and when a user is interacting with an AI system rather than a human. It came into force this month and applies to marketers running AI-driven campaigns or content into EU markets.
