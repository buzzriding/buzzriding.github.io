Opus Clip vs Repurpose.io vs Descript vs Munch: Video Repurposing Tools Compared (2026)

Most 'best content repurposing tools' lists mix clippers, distribution bots, and full editors into one ranking, as if they compete on the same job. They don't. Here's what four of the biggest names actually do, priced from their own pages.

The category problem: these tools don't do the same job

Search "content repurposing software" and the results blur three different jobs into one list: tools that clip a long recording into shorts, tools that distribute finished clips across platforms, and tools that edit raw footage from scratch.

We already covered the text-first side of this — turning a blog post into social copy — in our synthesis of AI content repurposing tools. That piece explicitly left out video and podcast tools because the workflow is different enough to need its own comparison. This is that comparison.

None of these four is a text-to-social tool. If your pillar content is a blog post, not a recording, the tools you want are Claude, ChatGPT, or Buffer's AI Assistant, covered in the linked piece above.

📚 What this is based on

Each vendor's own pricing and product pages, fetched directly in September 2026 and linked at each mention. Munch's figures come from a third-party pricing tracker, noted where used, since Munch's own pricing page did not return plan details on a direct fetch.

Opus Clip — the clipper, if your source is already recorded

Opus Clip's job is narrow and specific: feed it a long video, and it returns short, ranked clips scored for viral potential. Per Opus Clip's own pricing page, the Free plan includes 60 processing credits a month, watermarked exports, and 9:16 aspect ratio only. Starter runs $15/month for 150 credits with no annual option. Pro is $29/month, or $174 billed annually, and includes 3,600 annual credits plus two seats. Business is custom-priced with negotiated credits and seats.

Credits are spent per minute of source video processed, regardless of how many clips come out the other end. A 40-minute podcast costs 40 credits whether it yields one clip or fifteen.

What it doesn't do: Opus Clip only works from video you've already recorded. It doesn't write, distribute, or edit outside the clip itself. If your source is text, this tool has nothing to offer you.

Repurpose.io — distribution automation, not content creation

Repurpose.io sits in a different category entirely. Per Repurpose.io's own pricing page, fetched directly, the Starter plan is $35/month (or $349/year), connecting up to 3 accounts per social network with 5,000 published videos monthly. Pro is $79/month ($790/year) for 10 accounts per network and unlimited published videos. Agency runs $179/month ($1,790/year) for 25 accounts per network. A 14-day free trial publishes up to 10 videos with no credit card required.

The job here is syndication: content you've already published on one platform gets automatically resized, stripped of watermarks, and republished across TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Snapchat, Pinterest, and more, per the vendor's own feature list.

What it doesn't do: Repurpose.io doesn't generate new clips from a long recording. It moves and reformats content you feed it — it's an amplifier, not a creator.

Descript — the editor that repurposes as a side effect

Descript is a full text-based video and podcast editor, and repurposing is one feature among many rather than the whole product. Per Descript's own pricing page, plans are Free ($0, 1 media hour/month), Hobbyist ($16/month annual, $24 monthly, 10 media hours), Creator ($24/month annual, $35 monthly, 30 media hours — the vendor's "Most Popular" tier), Business ($50/month annual, $65 monthly, 40 media hours), and custom-priced Enterprise.

Descript's own feature list includes an AI tool explicitly labeled "Repurpose media: Create clips, highlights" across every paid tier, alongside full editing, transcription in 25 languages, and AI voice tools.

What it doesn't do: Descript's repurposing feature is bundled inside a much larger editor. If you only need clipping or only need distribution, you're paying for a lot of editing capability you may not touch.

Munch — clip volume tiers for teams, with a pricing caveat

Munch positions itself for brands and agencies repurposing long sessions — webinars, podcasts, and long-form video — at scale. Multiple third-party pricing trackers, including Techjury's Munch pricing page, report three tiers: Pro at roughly $49/month for around 200–250 upload minutes, Elite at roughly $116/month for 500 minutes, and Ultimate at roughly $220/month for around 1,000 minutes, with auto-cropping, AI captions, and no watermarks across tiers.

Why the caveat: Munch's own pricing page did not return plan details on a direct fetch during this research, so these figures should be verified against the live page before you commit to a plan. Every other tool in this piece is priced from the vendor's own page.

Doing the math on cost per minute of source video

Sticker prices don't tell you what a real workload costs. Using each vendor's own published numbers, here's the arithmetic for a clipper handling 250 minutes of source video a month — roughly eight 30-minute podcast episodes.

  • Opus Clip Pro (annual): $174/year ÷ 12 = $14.50/month for 300 credits, or about $0.048 per minute of source video processed, per Opus Clip's own credit system.
  • Munch Pro: roughly $49/month for around 200–250 minutes, or about $0.20–$0.245 per minute — four to five times Opus Clip's per-minute cost at this volume, per the third-party figures cited above.
  • Descript Creator (annual): $24/month for 30 media hours (1,800 minutes), or about $0.013 per minute — cheapest per minute, but that number includes a full editor, not just clipping.

This is simple division on published numbers, not a usage test — actual cost depends on how many clips you need per minute of source and how much manual cleanup each tool's output requires, which none of these vendor pages disclose.

Picking between them by job, not by "best"

These four don't compete for the same budget line. The real decision tree is about what you already have.

  • You record long video or podcasts and want short clips: Opus Clip or Munch. Compare cost per minute of source material at your actual volume, not the headline price.
  • You already have clips and want them everywhere automatically: Repurpose.io. It doesn't generate anything — it moves what you already made.
  • You want one tool that edits, transcribes, and clips: Descript. Costs more at the entry tier than a dedicated clipper, but replaces several tools if you're editing from scratch anyway.
  • Your pillar content is a blog post, not a recording: none of these four. See our text-first repurposing synthesis instead.

💡 The honest filter

Every one of these tools has a free trial or free tier. Before paying for any of them, run one real piece of content through the free option and count how much manual cleanup it needs. That thirty minutes tells you more than any comparison article, including this one.

What we're not claiming

We haven't run a controlled test comparing clip quality, caption accuracy, or output speed across these four tools on identical source material, and we're not inventing numbers to fill that gap. Pricing and feature claims above are sourced from each vendor's own current page, except Munch's, which relies on third-party trackers and should be independently verified.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between Opus Clip and Repurpose.io?
Opus Clip generates short clips from long video you upload, scored for viral potential. Repurpose.io doesn't generate clips — it automatically redistributes content you've already made across other platforms. Per their own pricing pages, they solve different problems and are often used together.
Is Descript worth it just for repurposing?
Only if you're also using its editing and transcription features. Per Descript's own pricing page, the entry paid tier starts at $16/month annually and includes full text-based editing alongside its clipping tool — that's a higher cost than a dedicated clipper if repurposing is the only feature you'll use.
Can I use these tools for free?
Opus Clip, Descript, and Repurpose.io all offer free tiers or trials per their own pricing pages: Opus Clip's Free plan includes 60 monthly credits, Descript's Free plan includes 1 media hour a month, and Repurpose.io's 14-day trial publishes up to 10 videos with no credit card. Munch's free-trial terms should be confirmed directly with the vendor.
Do any of these tools handle text-first content, like blog posts?
No. All four are built around video or audio as the source material. For turning a blog post into social and newsletter content, see our separate synthesis of text-first repurposing tools, which covers Claude, ChatGPT, Buffer's AI Assistant, Copy.ai, and Jasper.
Why isn't Munch's pricing sourced directly from its own page like the others?
A direct fetch of Munch's pricing page did not return plan details during this research. The figures cited here come from third-party pricing trackers instead, and are flagged as less reliable than the vendor-sourced numbers for Opus Clip, Repurpose.io, and Descript. Verify against Munch's live page before purchasing.
