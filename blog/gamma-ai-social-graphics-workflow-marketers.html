How to Build a Week of Social Graphics with Gamma AI (Free Workflow for Marketers)

This is a synthesis of Gamma's own Help Center documentation and TechCrunch's reporting on Gamma Imagine, not a BuzzRiding hands-on test. Gamma Imagine turns a text prompt into an on-brand graphic in a full creative workspace — here's what it actually does, and a practical 5-step workflow for batching a week of social visuals.

BR
The BuzzRiding TeamResearched and refined with AI tools

Why Gamma, and why now

Gamma built its name as an AI presentation tool. In March 2026 it shipped Gamma Imagine, a feature that generates standalone graphics — charts, social cards, infographics — from a single prompt, not just slides. Per TechCrunch's reporting, Gamma built Imagine explicitly to compete with Canva and Adobe, integrating with tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Zapier, and n8n to power data-driven asset generation.

That matters for marketers because most general-purpose AI image tools are built for illustration, not brand-consistent marketing assets. According to Gamma's own Help Center, Imagine lets you apply a saved workspace theme — logo, colours, fonts — to every graphic it generates, which is the part that usually eats the most time when building assets manually.

What follows is a synthesis of how the tool works, drawn from that documentation, plus a workflow for using it well — not a report of BuzzRiding having benchmarked it against Canva and Figma on a timed brief.

Step 1 · Load your brand kit once

Before generating anything, set up a Gamma workspace theme: logo, brand colours, and fonts. Gamma's Help Center confirms a saved theme's colours and typography carry into every Imagine generation you tie to it — skip this step and, by the tool's own design, results won't stay on-brand automatically.

If you already have a brand voice guide for AI tools, the same positioning and tone notes are worth keeping alongside your visual theme so both stay in sync.

Step 2 · Write one prompt per asset type, not per post

Don't prompt five separate times for five posts. Write one reusable prompt template per format instead — "quote card," "stat card," "announcement card" — and swap only the content inside it. Per Gamma's documentation, the more specific the prompt — content, tone, purpose — the better the result, and each graphic type comes with curated layout options to start from.

Example stat card prompt: "Create a social graphic in our brand style showing the statistic [X]. Bold number, one supporting line, minimal background." Reuse this exact structure for every stat post going forward.

📋 Reusable Prompt Template

"Create a [format] social graphic in our brand style. Headline: [text]. Supporting line: [text]. Keep it minimal — one focal point, no clutter. Aspect ratio: [1:1 for LinkedIn/X, 4:5 for Instagram]."

Step 3 · Generate in batches

Per Gamma's Help Center, Imagine lets you set the number of variations — up to three — before generating, producing multiple options to choose from in one pass rather than one generation at a time. Reviewing a small batch and picking the strongest result tends to be faster than trying to perfect a single generation through repeated prompts.

This mirrors the same batching principle behind our content repurposing workflow: produce more raw material upfront, curate afterward, rather than perfecting each piece in sequence.

Step 4 · Edit copy, not layout

Resist the urge to redesign from scratch. Gamma's canvas editor includes freeform text edits ("make the background darker," "change the title to say Q3 Results"), cropping, resizing, an AI eraser, and restyling — all documented in Gamma's Help Center — so refining an existing generation is usually faster than starting over. The highest-leverage manual edit is typically tightening the headline and checking it reads clearly at thumbnail size on a phone.

Every edit is saved as a new version with full history, per Gamma's documentation, so experimenting doesn't risk losing a result you liked.

Step 5 · Export and queue directly

Gamma's Help Center describes two ways to export a finished graphic — the Export button on the canvas, or the download button beneath the image — and once downloaded, the file works in any external tool that accepts images: Canva, PowerPoint, Google Slides, and social scheduling tools alike.

Pair this with a Claude project for captions, matched one-to-one with each graphic, so the whole batch — visual and copy — ships together instead of the graphics sitting finished while captions lag behind. See our Claude Projects templates for marketers for ready-to-copy caption prompts.

Why not just use a general image generator?

General image generators are strong at one-off, striking visuals but weaker at consistent marketing assets across a batch — each generation tends to reinterpret style from scratch rather than applying a fixed brand system. Imagine's theme mechanic, per Gamma's own documentation, is built specifically to keep fonts, colours, and layout logic consistent across every graphic generated under it.

That's a narrower tool by design: better suited to a repeatable weekly batch than to a single ambitious creative concept. If you need a single hero image with more artistic range, a general-purpose image model is still the better fit for that specific job.

None of these tools fully replace each other. Based on Gamma's own documentation and TechCrunch's reporting on Imagine's positioning against Canva and Adobe, here's how the three tend to be described for the specific job of weekly social graphics.

Where Gamma's documented limits are

Two limits are worth knowing before committing a weekly workflow to it. Gamma's Help Center notes that Imagine is not available on Business and Enterprise plans as of this writing, and that generating and editing images draws on Gamma credits — heavy weekly use will factor into plan costs. Separately, published reviews of Gamma's presentation exports note that PowerPoint exports of full decks can occasionally reflow text on download, worth double-checking before sending a client-ready file.

For a five-day batch of simple social graphics, neither limit is likely to matter much. For a full rebrand or a heavily designed client deck, a dedicated design tool still belongs in the loop.

Four more prompt templates worth saving

Once the stat card template is working, the same reusable-prompt approach extends naturally to the other formats Gamma's documentation lists as supported — including social media posts, posters, invites, and calendars.

  • Quote card: "Create a testimonial graphic in our brand style. Quote: [text]. Attribution: [name, title]. Simple, spacious layout, no more than one accent shape."
  • Before/after card: "Create a split-layout graphic comparing [before state] and [after state]. Clear visual separation, one metric highlighted per side."
  • Event countdown card: "Create a countdown graphic for [event name] on [date]. Bold date, one short line of context, brand colours only."
  • Listicle card: "Create a numbered list graphic with [N] items: [items]. Consistent spacing, numbers as the visual anchor."

Save these as a shared prompt library — a plain doc works fine — so nobody on the team starts from a blank prompt box. Consistency in the prompt is what produces consistency in the output.

Who this workflow is for

Best fit: solo marketers and small teams publishing several branded graphics a week without a dedicated designer. Less useful if you're producing polished, agency-level client decks — the documented export caveats above are more likely to cost time on that kind of project.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Gamma AI free to use for marketing graphics?
Gamma has a free tier that includes Imagine, per Gamma's own Help Center, sufficient to test the workflow. Imagine uses credits to generate and edit images and is available on all plans except Business and Enterprise; heavier weekly use typically means moving to a paid plan.
Can Gamma Imagine replace Canva for marketers?
For fast, on-brand social graphics generated from a prompt, it's a strong option — TechCrunch reported Gamma built Imagine specifically to compete with Canva and Adobe on this kind of asset. For highly customised layouts or print-ready files, Canva still offers broader manual editing control.
Does Gamma keep my brand colours and fonts consistent?
Yes. Gamma's Help Center confirms you can apply a saved Gamma theme — logo, colours, fonts — to an Imagine generation, and the theme influences the output. Skipping that setup step is the most common reason graphics drift off-brand.
What can I create with Gamma Imagine?
Per Gamma's documentation, Imagine supports infographics, posters, diagrams, social media posts, logos, invites, calendars, and org charts, plus an open-ended ‘something else’ option. You can generate up to three variations per prompt and refine results with freeform edits, cropping, resizing, and background removal.
When did Gamma launch Imagine?
TechCrunch reported the launch in March 2026, describing it as Gamma's move to compete more directly with Canva and Adobe on AI-generated marketing assets, integrated with tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Zapier.
