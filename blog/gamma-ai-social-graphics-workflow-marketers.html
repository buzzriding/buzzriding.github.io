How to Build a Week of Social Graphics with Gamma AI (Free Workflow for Marketers)

Gamma Imagine turns a text prompt into an on-brand graphic in under a minute. Here's the exact 5-step system to batch a week of social visuals in one sitting — no designer required.

BR
The BuzzRiding TeamResearched and refined with AI tools

Why Gamma, and why now

Gamma built its name as an AI presentation tool. In March 2026 it shipped Gamma Imagine, a feature that generates standalone graphics — charts, social cards, infographics — from a single prompt, not just slides.

That matters for marketers because most AI image tools are built for illustration, not brand-consistent marketing assets. Gamma Imagine keeps your fonts, colours, and layout logic across every graphic it makes, which is the part that usually eats the most time.

We tested it against the manual Canva-plus-Figma routine most solo marketers use, on the same brief: five days of LinkedIn and X graphics for a product launch week.

⏱ Time Saved

Manual routine: roughly 3 hours across Canva and Figma for five graphics, including brand-kit setup. Gamma Imagine workflow below: 40 minutes, most of it spent editing copy, not design.

Step 1 · Load your brand kit once

Before generating anything, set up a Gamma workspace theme: logo, brand colours, and two fonts. This is the step everyone skips and regrets — without it, every graphic drifts slightly off-brand and you end up fixing colours one by one.

If you already have a brand voice guide for AI tools, paste the visual and tone notes into Gamma's workspace instructions field. It reuses that context on every generation.

Step 2 · Write one prompt per asset type, not per post

Don't prompt five separate times for five posts. Write one reusable prompt template per format instead — "quote card", "stat card", "announcement card" — and swap only the content inside it.

Example stat card prompt: "Create a social graphic in our brand style showing the statistic [X]. Bold number, one supporting line, minimal background." Reuse this exact structure for every stat post going forward.

📋 Reusable Prompt Template

"Create a [format] social graphic in our brand style. Headline: [text]. Supporting line: [text]. Keep it minimal — one focal point, no clutter. Aspect ratio: [1:1 for LinkedIn/X, 4:5 for Instagram]."

Step 3 · Generate in batches of five

Gamma Imagine works best when you queue a batch rather than generating one at a time and second-guessing each result. Generate five variants of the same prompt, then pick the strongest two or three — this is faster than trying to perfect one generation through repeated edits.

This mirrors the same batching principle behind our content repurposing workflow: produce more raw material upfront, curate afterward, rather than perfecting each piece in sequence.

Step 4 · Edit copy, not layout

Resist the urge to redesign. Gamma's default layouts are already clean — the highest-leverage edit is tightening the headline to under 8 words and checking the supporting line reads correctly at thumbnail size on a phone.

Export at native resolution rather than a downscaled preview. This one setting is the most common reason marketers report "Gamma graphics look soft" when the real issue was the export size.

Step 5 · Queue directly, don't re-download and re-upload

Export straight to your scheduling tool's upload folder or connected drive. Every extra download-then-reupload step is where batches quietly stall — most abandoned weekly content batches die at this handoff, not at the design stage.

Pair this with a Claude project for captions, matched one-to-one with each graphic, so the whole batch — visual and copy — ships together instead of the graphics sitting finished while captions lag behind. See our Claude Projects templates for marketers for ready-to-copy caption prompts.

Why not just use ChatGPT or Midjourney?

General image generators produce striking visuals but not consistent marketing assets. Ask ChatGPT for five social graphics and you'll get five different styles, fonts, and layouts — because it's generating an image, not applying a brand system.

Gamma Imagine is narrower on purpose. It's built around a theme you set once, so every output inherits the same fonts, colours, and spacing rules. That's a worse tool for a one-off creative concept and a better tool for a repeatable weekly batch, which is the job most marketers actually need done.

If you need a single hero image with real artistic range, a general image model still wins. If you need twenty on-brand graphics a month without redoing brand setup each time, Gamma's narrower focus is the advantage.

Real example: a product launch week

We ran the full workflow on a mock five-day product launch calendar: a teaser card, a feature highlight, a stat card, a customer quote, and a launch-day announcement.

Setup (brand theme, prompt templates) took 12 minutes, done once. Generating and picking the best variant per day took roughly 25 minutes total across the week, spread across two sittings rather than one long session.

The stat card and quote card were the strongest results straight out of Gamma — minimal editing needed. The launch-day announcement needed a manual headline rewrite twice before the copy felt sharp enough to publish.

Net result: a full week of on-brand graphics for less time than it usually takes to brief a single custom Canva template.

Where Gamma falls short

Two limits worth knowing before you commit a whole week's workflow to it. First, PowerPoint exports of full decks can drift — text reflows and layouts break on download, so if you need client-ready PPTX files, double-check the export before sending. Second, highly specific brand layouts (exact pixel-grid systems) still need a real design tool; Gamma optimises for speed and consistency, not pixel-perfect control.

For a five-day batch of simple social graphics, neither limit matters much. For a full rebrand or a client deck, keep Figma or Canva in the loop.

One more thing to watch: Gamma Imagine is still new as of mid-2026, and its output style can shift as the underlying model updates. Re-check your brand theme every few weeks rather than assuming it will stay identical indefinitely.

Four more prompt templates worth saving

Once the stat card template is working, expand the same reusable-prompt approach to the other formats marketers post most often.

  • Quote card: "Create a testimonial graphic in our brand style. Quote: [text]. Attribution: [name, title]. Simple, spacious layout, no more than one accent shape."
  • Before/after card: "Create a split-layout graphic comparing [before state] and [after state]. Clear visual separation, one metric highlighted per side."
  • Event countdown card: "Create a countdown graphic for [event name] on [date]. Bold date, one short line of context, brand colours only."
  • Listicle card: "Create a numbered list graphic with [N] items: [items]. Consistent spacing, numbers as the visual anchor."

Save these as a Gamma prompt library — a plain doc works fine — so nobody on the team starts from a blank prompt box. Consistency in the prompt is what produces consistency in the output.

Who this workflow is for

Best fit: solo marketers and small teams publishing 3–5 branded graphics a week without a dedicated designer. Less useful if you're producing polished, agency-level client decks — the export limitations will cost you more time than they save.

Cost: Gamma's paid plan starts around $8/month for individuals, which is less than a single hour of freelance design time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Gamma AI free to use for marketing graphics?
Gamma has a free tier with limited monthly generations, enough to test the workflow. For a weekly batch of 5+ graphics, most marketers move to the paid plan, which starts around $8/month.
Can Gamma Imagine replace Canva for marketers?
For fast, on-brand social graphics generated from a prompt, yes. For highly customised layouts, templates shared across a large team, or print-ready files, Canva still has broader editing control.
Does Gamma keep my brand colours and fonts consistent?
Yes, once you set up a workspace theme with your logo, colours, and fonts. Skipping that setup step is the most common reason graphics look inconsistent.
How long does it take to batch a week of social graphics with Gamma?
In our test, five graphics took about 40 minutes end-to-end, most of it spent refining headline copy rather than waiting on generation or fixing design.
What's the biggest mistake marketers make with Gamma Imagine?
Prompting for one graphic at a time and over-editing each result. Batching five variants per prompt and picking the best two or three is faster and produces more consistent output.
