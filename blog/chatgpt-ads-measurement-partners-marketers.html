OpenAI Just Expanded ChatGPT Ads With Real Measurement Partners. Here's What the Early Numbers Show.

On 5 October 2026, OpenAI expanded ChatGPT Ads with a visual format and a long list of measurement partners. Here's what actually changed, who sees it, and what the first agencies running it are reporting.

BR
The BuzzRiding TeamResearched and refined with AI tools

ChatGPT has carried ads below conversations for months. On 5 October 2026, OpenAI announced something bigger. A new visual ad format, a long roster of measurement partners, and the first partner-reported performance numbers. This is the detail most coverage skimmed past.

Most write-ups this week repeated the headline: ads are coming to ChatGPT. Fewer dug into who actually sees them, or what the attribution partner list means in practice. Fewer still checked what agencies running real campaigns are finding once the invoices arrive.

🔬 How this was reviewed

Built from OpenAI's own announcement and its Ads in ChatGPT: the basics help-center article, plus independent reporting from Winbuzzer. No BuzzRiding account was enrolled as an advertiser, and nothing below is presented as something tested here. Every claim is attributed to OpenAI, a named partner, or the outlet that reported it.

What actually launched on 5 October

The new piece is a visual ad format, shown first during ChatGPT's image-generation flow. OpenAI says these ads stay clearly labeled and separate from the image a user is creating.

OpenAI also says ads never influence ChatGPT's answers. The claim is OpenAI's own, and no outlet reviewed here has independently verified it.

Rollout is a US-only test, starting later in October 2026, with a first group of advertisers. Businesses can apply at ads.openai.com. OpenAI has not specified which users land in the pilot or on what day it opens.

This isn't ChatGPT's first ad unit

The visual format is new. The underlying ad business is not. Per OpenAI's help-center documentation, ChatGPT has already been showing a simpler ad card below relevant conversations. That card carries an advertiser name, favicon, title, description, and a landing-page image.

Ad selection on that existing unit depends mainly on how relevant an ad is to a conversation's context and intent. Inputs include advertiser-supplied context hints, landing-page content, and ad copy, per the same documentation. Context hints aren't exact-match keywords, and OpenAI says they don't guarantee delivery.

So 5 October didn't launch ChatGPT advertising. It launched a measurement layer and a flashier format on top of an ad business that was already running.

It's also worth placing this next to the rest of the AI-search ad landscape. Google began testing ads inside AI Mode answers back in June 2025, over a year earlier. It has since expanded that test from mobile-only to desktop. OpenAI is not first to put ads inside an AI answer. It is, on this announcement, the first to publish a serious third-party measurement roster around one. For the bigger picture on how AI answer engines are reshaping visibility generally, see BuzzRiding's GEO guide.

Who actually sees a ChatGPT ad

This is the detail a marketer evaluating the platform needs first, and it's easy to miss in the announcement itself.

Per OpenAI's help-center article, ads do not show to anyone on a Plus, Pro, or Business plan. They also don't show to users ChatGPT identifies, or who are predicted, to be under 18.

The new visual format specifically targets Free and Go plan users. If your own customers use a paid ChatGPT tier, they are not the audience seeing this rollout yet.

The measurement partner list, decoded

The partner roster is the real substance of this announcement. It reads like alphabet soup unless you sort it by job:

  • Conversion data pipes: Hightouch, Tealium, and LiveRamp move a brand's own conversion data into OpenAI's system.
  • Attribution and reporting: AppsFlyer, Triple Whale, Adjust, DV Rockerbox, Northbeam, Branch, Singular, Kochava, Airbridge, and Tenjin handle click attribution and conversions-API reporting.
  • Full-funnel measurement: Fospha, Measured, and INCRMNTAL stitch ChatGPT ad exposure into a brand's wider funnel view.
  • Incrementality testing: Haus, Measured, and WorkMagic run early-stage geo-based experiments. The goal is isolating what ads actually caused, versus what would have happened anyway.

In plain terms: OpenAI wants ChatGPT Ads plugged into the same measurement stack marketers already use for Google and Meta. That's a meaningful step past a basic ad unit with no reporting.

The three numbers OpenAI is showing off

OpenAI's announcement cites three partner-reported results, each from a different measurement partner:

  • WeightWatchers: an attributed cost per acquisition 15.3% below its blended paid-search benchmark, per DV Rockerbox.
  • Dose: a statistically significant lift, with 67% of incremental purchases from net-new customers, per WorkMagic.
  • Portland Leather: 93% of ChatGPT Ads visitors were new to the brand, per Triple Whale.

These are real, named, partner-attributed figures, not invented ones. They are also exactly the three cases OpenAI chose to publish. None of the three disclose campaign budget, flight dates, or how each metric was defined. Treat them as a vendor's best examples, not a representative average.

What agencies already running it are finding

This is the part the launch-day coverage mostly skipped. It's also the most useful section for deciding how much to trust this platform right now.

Per Winbuzzer's reporting, Accuracast's CEO Farhad Divecha said a UK campaign generated completed lead forms. Two weeks later, ChatGPT's own reporting still showed zero conversions for it. The agency also logged roughly 100 clicks in ChatGPT's dashboard against 20 in its own tracking.

Divecha called unpredictable delivery the agency's biggest problem. Thousands of impressions landed one week, under 100 the next, with no clear cause. Some advertisers reportedly withheld conversion-tracking data entirely, citing unfamiliarity with OpenAI's data terms next to Google's and Meta's.

Not every agency reports the same friction. Executives at AdRoll and Jellyfish said they hadn't seen comparable click discrepancies. Jellyfish's Victor Batista called the shift toward cost-per-acquisition bidding a meaningful step forward as more partner data accumulates.

Brand safety is still being built, not finished

OpenAI is piloting brand suitability checks with DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science. The goal is keeping ads out of sensitive or emotionally vulnerable conversations. Qualifying advertisers can also set Negative Phrases to block specific placements.

DoubleVerify's framing, per OpenAI's announcement, is that a conversation is the context for a ChatGPT ad, not a static page. That's a genuinely different brand-safety problem than anything built for search or social. It's also still in controlled testing rather than general release.

What this means if you're not running a seven-figure media budget

Three practical takeaways, based strictly on what's published above:

  • Don't expect Google Ads-level measurement maturity yet. The partner list is real, but the reported tracking gaps suggest the plumbing is still being tightened.
  • Check which plan your own customers use. If your audience leans Plus or Pro, this specific rollout doesn't reach them yet.
  • Apply early if you want to learn the platform, budget small if you do. Signing up at ads.openai.com costs nothing but time. Early access is also how you'd see the measurement gaps Accuracast described, firsthand.

💡 Key takeaway

OpenAI's 5 October update is a real step toward ad-platform maturity: a visual format, a serious measurement-partner roster, and named case studies. It is not yet a mature, predictable ad platform. Agencies running real budgets are finding tracking gaps alongside the good numbers.

The obvious caveat

This piece is built from OpenAI's own announcement and documentation, plus one independent report citing agency sources. No account here ran a ChatGPT Ads campaign, so none of this is presented as first-hand testing. Every figure above traces to OpenAI, a named measurement partner, or an agency quoted by the outlet linked. Verify current terms directly at ads.openai.com before budgeting against anything here, since early-access ad platforms change their rules fast.

Frequently Asked Questions

What did OpenAI actually announce on 5 October 2026?
A visual ad format shown during image generation, a broad list of new measurement partners, and three partner-reported case studies.
Who sees ads in ChatGPT?
Only users on the Free and Go plans, per OpenAI's help-center documentation. Plus, Pro, and Business plan users do not see ads. The system also doesn't show ads to users identified or predicted to be under 18.
How do I sign up to advertise on ChatGPT?
Businesses can apply at ads.openai.com. OpenAI describes October's rollout as a US-only test opening to a first group of advertisers. It has not published a start date for new applicants.
Is ChatGPT Ads measurement reliable yet?
It's improving but not mature. Winbuzzer reported that agency Accuracast saw roughly 100 clicks in ChatGPT's dashboard against 20 in its own system. It also saw unpredictable week-to-week delivery. Other agencies, including AdRoll and Jellyfish, reported not seeing the same gaps.
Are the WeightWatchers, Dose, and Portland Leather results representative?
They're real, named, partner-attributed figures. But OpenAI chose to publish exactly these three, and none disclose budget, campaign dates, or metric definitions. Treat them as the platform's best examples, not an expected average.
Is this the first time AI chat or search tools have shown ads?
No. ChatGPT already showed a simpler text-and-image ad card before this update. Google began testing ads inside AI Mode answers in June 2025, more than a year earlier. OpenAI's October 2026 update is a measurement and format upgrade, not the start of AI advertising.
